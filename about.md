Leverage Lab was built by [Bill Eisenhauer](https://portfolio.billeisenhauer.com) as an executable management argument.

Software organizations often discuss leverage through the output of specialized teams: frameworks released, AI tools adopted, migrations completed, or external capacity purchased. Those measures can conceal whether the whole system became more capable.

The simulator asks a different question:

> After the intervention, did more work become accepted, used, and durably owned—and did we notice where the constraint moved next?

It combines ideas from flow management, constraint theory, internal-platform product management, and risk-tiered human-agent delivery. It is deliberately company-neutral and contains no private organizational information.

The project is an exploration, not a proprietary methodology or a prescription. Its value is in making assumptions visible enough to test.

## A work sample—and an invitation

Leverage Lab is also a sample of how Bill works: turn an ambiguous leadership question into visible assumptions, a small executable model, and a learning loop that people can challenge together.

Bill is exploring engineering leadership work at the intersection of platform and foundations, developer experience, human-agent delivery, and organization-scale leverage. If those are live questions in your organization—or this experiment gives you a better way to frame one—he would be glad to compare notes.
