What the factory reports

You see defects the checks caught and incidents that have already reached customers. Escapes still in production don’t count yet, so the catch rate flatters the checks.

02 · Decide

Which rooms run dark?

Lit rooms wait for you to read every change. Dark rooms merge when the checks pass, and you glance at a list later.

03 · Invest

Build checks and habits

Checks stay in place once built. Measuring and drills are one-off and can be repeated. Eight points per cycle; unspent points don’t carry over.

Field guide · 90 seconds

How to play

Your goal: ship as much value as you can while causing as little harm as you can, counting harm that hasn’t surfaced yet.

Easy or Hard? Easy mode shows the defects still waiting in production and runs each choice through 24 futures. Hard mode shows only what a real factory would show you.

  1. 1
    Read the report
    Value
    Merged changes, weighted by what they’re worth. A feature counts more than a docs fix.
    Harm
    Escaped defects, weighted by consequence. A broken page is 1–3; wrong charges or exposed accounts are 10–20.
    Catch rate
    What the checks caught, against incidents so far. It looks better than it is until the late defects arrive.
  2. 2
    Light or darken rooms

    Dark rooms ship without your time. Each dark merge also adds code nobody has read, which makes that room’s later defects more likely and worse.

  3. 3
    Spend up to 8 points

    Each check catches some kinds of defect and not others. Know which.

  4. 4
    Predict and run

    Pick the room you think will produce the costliest incident, or none. Six weeks run.

  5. 5
    Read the debrief

    After four cycles, your decisions replay in 200 futures, against reading everything and reading nothing.

Words you’ll see

Lit room
You read every change before it merges. Safer, but it costs your time, and a long queue makes you skim.
Dark room
Changes merge when the automated checks pass. You glance at a list afterward.
Unread code
Changes nobody read. The more a room has, the more often its later changes break, and the worse its incidents are.
Lapse rule
When a dark room causes an incident, it goes back to lit at once. You can make it dark again next cycle.
Defects still in production
Bugs that have merged but haven’t hurt anyone yet. They will. Only Easy mode and the debrief show them.
Futures
The same decisions replayed with different luck. One run can be lucky; 200 show the range.

Easy-mode labels

Helpful / harmful
Over the rest of the run, value minus harm goes up or down.
Looks good now, costs later
The next six weeks improve, and the run ends worse. Watch for this one.
Pays on average, worse worst case
Usually a gain, but the unlucky futures get much worse.
Learning value
No protection, only knowledge. Measuring the checks is the example.
Reading tip

Ask two questions of each room: how well can the checks tell good work from bad, and how much does a mistake there cost? Stuck? “Give me a hint” under the factory’s report goes from a general tip to what the model expects.

The argument behind the model

Autonomy is earned room by room.

01

Bad news arrives late.

Most escaped defects surface weeks after they merge. A factory judged on its first month is judged before the evidence exists.

02

Consequence isn’t size.

A one-line bump to the package your sign-in runs on is small and critical. Rooms are drawn by what a mistake costs, not by how big the change looks.

03

Measure the checks, too.

The rate a factory reports is the rate it can see. Replaying known defects through the checks is the only way to learn what they actually catch, and it goes stale when they change.
